This sample demonstrates how word origins affect the "feel" of prose.
Germanic words (amber) feel direct and visceral.
Latin words (blue) feel formal and intellectual.
Greek words (purple) feel scholarly and technical.

Try pressing Ctrl+I on any word to inspect its etymology annotation!


PASSAGE 1: Germanic Style (direct, visceral, earthy)
----------------------------------------------------

The man walked through the cold night. His heart beat fast. Fear gripped
his gut. The wind howled and bit at his skin. He thought of home, of
fire and warmth, of his wife and children asleep in their beds.

He had to go on. Death followed him. The dark wood stretched out before
him, endless and deep. Trees stood like ghosts in the moonlight. An owl
called out. Something stirred in the brush.

He ran. His feet pounded the hard earth. Blood rushed in his ears. Breath
came sharp and fast. He did not look back. He could not stop.


PASSAGE 2: Latinate Style (formal, intellectual, abstract)
----------------------------------------------------------

The individual proceeded through the nocturnal environment. His cardiac
function accelerated considerably. Apprehension dominated his emotional
state. The atmospheric conditions presented significant challenges to his
physical comfort.

He contemplated his residence, the thermal regulations and familial
relationships that characterized his domestic situation. His spouse and
offspring remained in a state of repose.

Continuation was imperative. Mortality pursued his position. The arboreal
region extended infinitely, demonstrating considerable depth. The vegetation
assumed spectral qualities in the lunar illumination.


PASSAGE 3: Mixed (natural prose uses both)
------------------------------------------

The old professor sat in his study, surrounded by books. He had spent
his life in pursuit of knowledge, searching for truth in ancient texts.

His students often asked him: what is the secret to understanding?

He would smile and say: "Use simple words when you can. The heart knows
what the mind cannot explain. But when you need to be precise, when the
idea demands it, then reach for the longer words. They carry the weight
of centuries of thought."

Good writing, he believed, was a dance between the gut and the intellect,
between the earthy and the abstract. Neither alone was sufficient.


PASSAGE 4: Technical/Greek (scholarly, scientific)
--------------------------------------------------

The analysis of the phenomenon required systematic methodology. The
hypothesis suggested a paradigm shift in our understanding of atomic
physics. Through careful diagnosis, the researchers identified the
underlying mechanism.

Philosophy and psychology both contributed to the theoretical framework.
The synthesis of biology and chemistry opened new avenues of investigation.
Mathematical models provided the basis for experimental validation.
